## Questions

### 1. The analyst's stance and the two tangible outputs

Chapter 01 names the analyst's stance across the module and the two tangible outputs the rest of the module instruments. Which statement captures the chapter's framing?

- A. The analyst's stance is control-library designer — authoring the master list of controls, their wording, their cadence, and their required evidence class; chapter 02 is the authoring rubric.
- B. The analyst's stance is cycle runner and reporter — reading the master control library `senior-ai-governance-architect` publishes, running the periodic test cycle against it, pulling evidence from ML / infra / product owners, attaching it to the GRC-platform records, flagging stale evidence, tagging security-linked records, composing the control-status report, running the third-party AI vendor / model / dataset intake in parallel, and maintaining the analyst-tier crosswalk. The two tangible outputs are the control-status report and the vendor-risk-register row set; everything else in the module is instrumental to producing those two correctly.
- C. The analyst's stance is internal auditor — issuing the opinion on operating effectiveness directly to the audit committee; chapter 03 is the opinion rubric.
- D. The analyst's stance is interchangeable with `ai-infra-security` — the two roles co-author the exploit assessment, the mitigation code, and the control report.

### 2. The cycle, the evidence-artefact classes, and the stale-evidence flag rules

Chapter 02 fixes the four cycle phases, the six evidence-artefact classes, and the four stale-evidence flag rules. Which statement matches the chapter?

- A. The cycle has two phases (gather and summarise), the evidence is free-form prose per control, and *stale* is a judgment call the analyst makes after re-reading each file.
- B. The cycle has four phases — *scope* (apply the scoping rule to select the in-period subset of the master library), *pull* (request evidence per control from the named owner on a deadline), *attach and test* (record each artefact in the GRC platform with a stable `EV-<year>-<sequence>` identifier, time window, pulled-on date, pulled-by actor, and content pin, then assign the verdict), *report and flag* (roll into the status report and open follow-ups on stale or failing rows). The six evidence-artefact classes are config export, log sample, eval-report link, sign-off record, screenshot, and interview note; each control record names the class it expects, and delivering a weaker class is an *evidence-class downgrade* finding, not a normal outcome. The four stale-evidence flag rules — cadence-window, config-change, model-version-change, regulatory-obligation-change — each independently trip a re-test, and the analyst applies the rules rather than deciding them (the rules are policy set by `senior-ai-governance-architect`).
- C. The cycle has three phases (scope, pull, and attach); reporting is a GRC-platform dashboard export rather than its own phase; stale evidence is tolerated as long as the next scheduled test will cover it.
- D. The six evidence-artefact classes are universal, so the control record never specifies which class to expect; and the pull protocol runs by posting a single enterprise-wide spreadsheet of asks that the analyst collects responses to after the quarter closes.

### 3. The four-verdict discipline and the `CTL-<year>-<sequence>-<test-N>` row

Chapter 03 fixes the row schema and the verdict language. Which statement matches the chapter?

- A. The row is a free-text paragraph per control; the analyst softens verdicts to *mostly-pass* or *pass with reservations* where evidence is mixed, and internal audit interprets the softening.
- B. The row is a twenty-column test record per control per test (identity across test ID, control ID, control statement verbatim from the library, applicable `INV-` systems, owner, cadence, test-in-period date, verdict, evidence class expected vs delivered, attached `EV-` identifiers, stale-evidence flags, prior-period verdict, trend, follow-up action, escalation class, satisfied `REG-` obligations, framework crosswalk pointers, analyst note, and analyst). The verdicts are four words: *pass* (evidence in the expected class, within cadence, consistent with the control statement, no stale flag), *fail* (evidence missing or inconsistent — follow-up mandatory), *deviation* (evidence present but shaped differently than the statement expects — follow-up mandatory), and *not-tested-in-period* (deferred per cadence, reason and next scheduled date recorded). Anything else is a report-formatting failure, not a fifth verdict.
- C. The row omits the trend column because computing the trend against prior periods invites bias into the verdict.
- D. The row records the analyst's opinion on operating effectiveness; internal audit countersigns the opinion as a formality.

### 4. Operating-effectiveness language — what the analyst writes and does not write

Chapter 03 draws a specific line around *operating effectiveness* phrasing. Which statement captures the chapter's discipline?

- A. The analyst writes *"operating effectively"* in the executive summary whenever the pass count exceeds the fail count; this is the headline the auditor expects.
- B. The analyst writes *"tested with expected evidence class; passed; no stale-evidence flag tripped"* when the row supports it, *"tested with downgraded evidence class (expected X; delivered Y). Follow-up filed to `senior-ai-governance-architect` to redesign the control toward a produceable class"* when the row calls for it, and *"prior-period verdict was fail; follow-up action was completed on [date]; current-period verdict is pass; trend is improved"* when the row carries a completed follow-up — but does *not* write *"operating effectively,"* *"operates in principle,"* or *"now working correctly."* *Operating effectively* is internal audit's opinion, not an analyst input; the analyst supplies the rows, internal audit forms the opinion.
- C. The analyst never writes any interpretive language — every row is a single column of numeric verdicts, and all narrative stays out of the report.
- D. The analyst records the control-library designer's self-assessment of effectiveness verbatim in the row; this is the point of the pre-issuance review with `senior-ai-governance-architect`.

### 5. OWASP LLM Top 10 tagging — the five constraints

Chapter 04 fixes a one-line tagging rule plus five constraints. Which statement matches the chapter?

- A. The tag is a severity claim; the analyst assigns Critical / High / Medium / Low per OWASP entry number (LLM01 is always High, LLM10 is always Low), and `ai-infra-security` reads the severity directly off the tag.
- B. The tag is the analyst's *filing convention* for security-linked control records and findings — not a security opinion. Constraints: tag by the *primary* category with one or two secondaries in the analyst note where multiple items touch; cite the version (*LLM01:2025*, not *LLM01*) and raise a library-level review request when OWASP ships a new edition; tag *findings* (and the control records and tickets that carry them), not *systems* (`mod-102` inventory fields do the system-level anchor); do not tag when the finding is not security-linked (e.g., a demographic-slice underperformance surfaced via `mod-104` is not *LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning* absent evidence of a deliberate poisoning vector); and do not use the tag as a severity claim (severity is `ai-infra-security`'s call in context). Speculative tagging is not tagging — a tag follows evidence, not hypothesis.
- C. The tag lives on the inventory row, not on the finding; a system that has had five prompt-injection findings carries one aggregate LLM01 tag on its `INV-<year>-<sequence>` row.
- D. Version citation is cosmetic — *LLM01* and *LLM01:2025* mean the same thing; the analyst does not need to re-read the list when OWASP revises it.

### 6. The four references — OWASP, NIST AI 100-2, MITRE ATLAS, Google SAIF

Chapter 05 fixes what each of the four references does and how the analyst composes them. Which statement captures the chapter?

- A. The four references are redundant; the analyst picks one and ignores the others. ATLAS is the shortest, so default to it.
- B. The four references do different work. OWASP LLM Top 10 is the analyst's *primary filing convention* for security-linked findings (chapter 04). NIST AI 100-2 is the *attack-class taxonomy* that names the four families — evasion, poisoning, privacy, abuse — and anchors the *why-we-tag-what-we-tag* justification the analyst cites (edition-cited, e.g., `AI 100-2e2025 — Evasion`). MITRE ATLAS is the *adversarial-tactics-and-techniques matrix* (ATT&CK-shaped, AI-populated) whose technique identifiers (e.g., `AML.T0051`) the analyst carries forward when the security team's source artefact names one — the analyst does not derive identifiers from scratch. Google SAIF is a *defender-oriented programme framework* naming six areas of AI-security work the enterprise's controls anchor to; SAIF labels controls, not findings, and treating SAIF as a threat taxonomy is a named failure mode. The analyst carries whatever the source artefact provides and does not manufacture the missing references.
- C. SAIF is a threat taxonomy equivalent to OWASP; the analyst can tag a finding *SAIF-2* in place of an OWASP identifier.
- D. ATLAS technique identification is analyst-tier work — the analyst reads a finding and derives the `AML.T<n>` identifier from scratch when the source report does not name one.

### 7. Vendor intake — the six evidence artefacts and the inheritance decision

Chapter 06 fixes the six vendor-evidence artefact classes, the twenty-two-column `VND-<year>-<sequence>` row, and the inheritance-decision vocabulary. Which statement matches the chapter?

- A. The intake reads one artefact per vendor (whichever the vendor publishes most prominently); the row has a free-form notes field and no fixed columns; and the analyst signs the DPA to speed the renewal cycle.
- B. The intake reads six artefact classes per vendor — model card (or system card), sub-processor list, DPA, ISO/IEC 42001 certification letter (with certifying-body accreditation verified against the IAF signatory list), adjacent attestations plus independent audit report, and security questionnaire response — and records each on the twenty-two-column row even when the vendor did not produce it (recording *not-published* / *not-conducted* / *not-applicable* is more useful than omitting the class). The inheritance decision is one of three: *inherit* (accept vendor controls; minimal compensating controls), *inherit-with-compensating-controls* (list the enterprise `CTL-<year>-<sequence>` identifiers that close the gap), or *do-not-inherit* (vendor should not be used until specific gaps close). The analyst reads and rows; `procurement` / `legal` / `data-protection-officer` decide the contract, DPA, and personal-data-processing posture — the analyst does not redline, sign, or accept.
- C. Inheritance decisions are a four-level scale (full / partial / conditional / none); the analyst sets the level and procurement countersigns.
- D. A ForHumanity-linked audit substitutes for an ISO/IEC 42001 certification letter on the vendor row; chapter 06 columns 12 (certification) and 14 (independent audit) are the same field.

### 8. The crosswalk vs the master control library

Chapter 07 distinguishes the analyst-tier crosswalk from the master control library and fixes the cross-walking discipline. Which statement captures the chapter?

- A. The crosswalk *is* the master control library, maintained by the analyst as a single artefact that the architect reviews periodically.
- B. The crosswalk is an analyst-tier *projection* of the master control library onto five framework columns — ISO/IEC 42001 clauses, NIST AI RMF sub-categories, EU AI Act articles, SR 11-7 pillars, and the internal policy catalogue — plus optional overlays (NIST AI 600-1 risk-action identifiers, ISO/IEC 23894 risk-source references, sector-specific guidance, voluntary framework anchors). The row is eleven columns of pointers, not a duplicate of the library. The analyst walks four patterns — regulation-forward, control-forward, framework-forward, inventory-forward — and files four gap classes (uncovered obligation, uncovered control, mis-anchored control, drift) as class-1 library-design-fix escalations. The architect owns the master library; the analyst's line is *suggests, re-populates after the architect decides* — the analyst never edits the library from the crosswalk side.
- C. The crosswalk is a one-column mapping (internal control → ISO/IEC 42001 clause) and all other frameworks live on separate artefacts.
- D. Framework-forward walks are out of scope for the analyst — only the architect can walk the external framework onto the internal library.

### 9. ForHumanity, BABL AI, and the NYC Local Law 144 through-line

Chapter 08 positions the two independent-audit-adjacent bodies the analyst most commonly encounters and names the through-line where they cross the enterprise's inbox. Which statement captures the chapter?

- A. ForHumanity and BABL AI are IAF-accredited certification bodies whose attestations substitute directly for ISO/IEC 42001 certification; the analyst records them in the certification column of the vendor row.
- B. ForHumanity is a US-registered public charity that publishes openly reviewed *audit criteria* (the ForHumanity Body of Knowledge), maintains the Independent Audit of AI Systems (IAAIS) methodology, and runs an auditor credentialing programme. BABL AI is a US consultancy and training organisation that both delivers algorithmic-audit services (including bias audits in employment contexts) and runs practitioner training with completion credentials. Neither is an IAF-recognised accreditation body in the sense that accredits certifying bodies for ISO management-system certification; a ForHumanity- or BABL-linked audit is a distinct evidence class — valuable and recordable — that does not substitute for an ISO/IEC 42001 certificate. The through-line where both names cross the analyst's inbox is NYC Local Law 144's bias-audit obligation (independent bias audit within one year of AEDT use, plus a required publication of the audit summary on the employer's website); the pattern generalises to the Colorado AI Act and other jurisdictional AI-audit obligations. On the vendor row and the control test, the analyst records *criteria origin* (which criteria set, with version) and *auditor credentialing* (which credential the auditor holds) as distinct fields — conflating them is a named failure mode.
- C. The analyst personally audits the enterprise's own systems where they hold a ForHumanity or BABL credential; chapter 08 names this as the analyst-tier path to internal-audit independence.
- D. Local Law 144 only requires the audit to be conducted; publishing a summary on the employer's website is a nice-to-have outside the obligation.

### 10. The four escalation classes

Chapter 09 defines four escalation classes with specific triggers, owners, and shapes. Which statement captures the chapter?

- A. There is one escalation flow — the analyst emails the governance council whenever a control fails — and the class language is not part of the discipline.
- B. There are four classes. *Class 1 — library-design fix needed* routes to `senior-ai-governance-architect` and triggers on repeated structural failure of a control (evidence class not produceable, statement ambiguous, owner unassigned, cadence unrealistic, sampling rule missing) or on a crosswalk gap (uncovered obligation, uncovered control, mis-anchored control, drift); the analyst does not rewrite the control. *Class 2 — security route to `ai-infra-security`* carries the OWASP / AI 100-2 / ATLAS / SAIF tag set per chapters 04–05, the affected `INV-` systems, and the evidence link, with ML / infra / product owners copied; the analyst does not set severity, reproduce the exploit, draft mitigation code, or close the finding on their own assessment. *Class 3 — vendor / contract action* routes to `procurement` / `legal` (and `data-protection-officer` where personal data is in scope, `ai-infra-security` where security is in scope) and carries the `VND-<year>-<sequence>` identifier, the surfaced column, and the requested action; the analyst does not redline, negotiate, sign, or pause unilaterally. *Class 4 — governance-council decision* convenes `head-of-ai-governance` and the council on findings that cross functional lines (residual-risk acceptance beyond a named threshold, enterprise-wide policy change, library re-design across business units, incident remediation across functions); the analyst supplies context plus the architect's recommendation, and does not take the decision or downgrade to a lower class to avoid the slot. Every entry sits in the `ESC-<year>-<sequence>` register with a persistent row shape; resolution requires a resolving artefact identifier (verbal commitments do not close escalations); and the roll-up feeds §5 of the control-status report plus the council briefings in `mod-110`.
- C. All escalations are confidential and are not referenced in the control-status report — the analyst keeps the register separate to protect the specialists it names.
- D. Escalations resolve on a verbal commitment from the owning team; recording a resolving artefact identifier is optional.
