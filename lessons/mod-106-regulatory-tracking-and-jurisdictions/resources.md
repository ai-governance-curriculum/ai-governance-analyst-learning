# Resources — mod-106 Cross-Jurisdiction Regulatory Tracking

Primary references for every jurisdictional branch and sector overlay the module walks. Read the statute or the regulator's own published document first; practitioner briefings and law-firm client alerts are useful for *noticing* a change quickly but do not sit in the tracker's *primary-source URLs* column — the analyst chases the primary reference before writing the row.

## The tracker as method and the AIGP shared vocabulary

Chapter 01 and chapter 08 orientation references.

- [ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) — the AI management-system standard. Clause 6 requires an AI risk assessment and AI system impact assessment; internal-audit findings on tracker staleness typically cite Clause 9.2 (internal audit) and Clause 9.3 (management review) against a tracker whose *last-verified* dates are older than the review cadence.
- [ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 (Artificial Intelligence)](https://www.iso.org/committee/6794475.html) — the SC 42 committee page; useful for tracking work items that will attach to the tracker (new AI standards in development, amendments to 42001 / 42005 / 23894).
- [IAPP AIGP certification page and body of knowledge](https://iapp.org/certify/aigp/) — the AIGP certification and BoK; the analyst uses the domain structure to tag tracker rows for audience-filtered reads (chapter 08).
- [IAPP Resource Center — AI governance](https://iapp.org/resources/) — the IAPP-maintained hub for AI governance topic guides, tracker projects, and practitioner briefings.
- [IAPP Global AI Legislation Tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/global-ai-legislation-tracker/) — the IAPP-maintained global tracker; secondary reference, useful for cross-checking whether an obligation the analyst has just noticed is already listed and — if so — how IAPP has characterised it.
- [IAPP US State AI Governance Legislation Tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-ai-governance-legislation-tracker/) — the IAPP-maintained US state tracker; same use pattern.
- [IAPP US State Privacy Legislation Tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-legislation-tracker/) — the IAPP-maintained comprehensive-consumer-privacy tracker; used alongside the AI tracker where a state-privacy statute covers automated-decisionmaking or profiling.
- [OECD.AI Policy Observatory](https://oecd.ai/) — the OECD-maintained AI-policy hub; useful for cross-jurisdictional context and for the [OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles) baseline cited from voluntary-code chapters.

## The EU stack — chapter 02

The AI Act, GDPR, and the Council of Europe Framework Convention.

- [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — the EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) — consolidated text on EUR-Lex; the authoritative source for Articles 5, 6, 26, 27, 50, 51–56, 70, 88, 113 and Annex III.
- [artificialintelligenceact.eu — annotated Act mirror](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/) — cross-referenced annotated mirror of the Act; convenient for citing specific articles with narrative context. Not authoritative; use only for reader navigation and cross-check against EUR-Lex.
- [European Commission — European AI Office](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office) — the AI Act enforcement and template-publishing body; publishes the Article 27(3) FRIA notification template, the [GPAI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-code-practice), and ongoing Commission guidance under the AI Act.
- [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — GDPR (consolidated text)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) — the authoritative EUR-Lex text; Articles 5, 6, 9, 22, 30, 35, and 36 are the AI-relevant hooks.
- [gdpr-info.eu — annotated GDPR full text](https://gdpr-info.eu/) — annotated hypertext of the GDPR; useful for cross-referencing between articles.
- [Article 29 Working Party — WP 248 rev.01: Guidelines on Data Protection Impact Assessment](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/611236) — the nine-criteria filter for the Article 35 DPIA trigger; endorsed by the EDPB.
- [Article 29 Working Party — WP 251 rev.01: Guidelines on Automated individual decision-making and Profiling](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/612053) — the *meaningful human involvement* framing for Article 22.
- [European Data Protection Board (EDPB) — Guidelines, Recommendations and Best Practices](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/general-guidance/gdpr-guidelines-recommendations-best-practices_en) — the EDPB's ongoing guidance surface; check for guidelines that update or supplement WP 248 / WP 251 before authoring a flag block.
- [Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law](https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence) — the CoE framework convention; opened for signature 5 September 2024 at Vilnius. Track ratifications and deposit of instruments of ratification against the enterprise's operating jurisdictions.
- [Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (2012/C 326/02)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12012P/TXT) — the Charter rights the AI Act Article 27 FRIA attaches to.

## What is deliberately not here

- Law-firm client alerts, consultancy trend reports, and vendor "trust centre" one-pagers — useful for *noticing* a change quickly, but the tracker's *primary-source URLs* column carries the regulator's own published document. The analyst chases the primary reference before writing the row.
- Unofficial consolidated texts of the AI Act, GDPR, or Colorado AI Act — use EUR-Lex, EUR-Lex, and the Colorado General Assembly respectively for citations that will appear in audit-facing artefacts. Annotated mirrors (like artificialintelligenceact.eu and gdpr-info.eu) are useful for reader navigation but are not authoritative.
- Case dockets, plaintiff-bar filings, and enforcement-action press coverage — relevant to counsel's risk-management view, not to the tracker's primary-source column. The analyst records the regulator's own enforcement bulletins; case-law characterisation belongs to counsel.
- Snapshots of the AIGP body-of-knowledge taken more than one IAPP revision cycle ago — IAPP revises the BoK on a periodic basis; cite against the current published BoK on the AIGP page, not against an older summary.
- Sponsor-produced "voluntary code compliance" attestations that do not cite the code's chapter and clause — a voluntary code adherence claim without evidence of the specific chapters the enterprise attests to is a marketing statement, not evidence for the tracker.
