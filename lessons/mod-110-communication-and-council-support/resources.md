# Resources — mod-110 Stakeholder Communication, Council Support, and Policy Drafting at the Analyst Tier

Primary references for the standards that fix board-level governance vocabulary the analyst writes into, the council-governance and minutes-and-records practice the analyst's meeting-packet work sits inside, the internal AI policy landscape the analyst's first-cut drafts route through, the external-facing responsible-AI-communication references the analyst factual-reviews against, and the certification bodies whose credentials the analyst's own portfolio plan (chapter 08) tracks. Read the primary source (the standard's authoring body, the regulator's own text, the credential's exam blueprint) first; secondary summaries, consultancy briefings, and vendor pages help notice a change but do not substitute for the citation on a briefing to legal, on a policy first cut, or on an external artefact under legal review.

## ISO/IEC standards the module leans on directly — chapters 03, 06, 07, 08

The AI-governance ISO/IEC family (JTC 1/SC 42 — Artificial Intelligence) whose vocabulary the analyst uses across board briefings, first-cut policy drafts, and external artefacts.

- [ISO/IEC 38507:2022 — Information technology — Governance of IT — Governance implications of the use of artificial intelligence by organizations](https://www.iso.org/standard/56641.html) — the standard chapter 03 uses as the vocabulary anchor for board-level briefings. Guidance (not certifiable). Introduces the *governance body / management* distinction and the *evaluate / direct / monitor* triad the analyst structures the board pack around.
- [ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) — the certifiable AI management system standard. Cited in the model-approval standard, the third-party AI vendor review standard, and the external-facing vocabulary discipline on *certified* vs *aligned* vs *implementing*. Annex A carries the AI-specific control set the enterprise's control library aligns to (`mod-108`).
- [ISO/IEC 23894:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Guidance on risk management](https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html) — the risk-management guidance the analyst borrows vocabulary from inside the risk-appetite-and-portfolio sections of the board briefing.
- [ISO/IEC 42005:2025 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — AI system impact assessment](https://www.iso.org/standard/44545.html) — the impact-assessment standard cited from `mod-105`; referenced in the council pre-read when an IA / FRIA / DPIA sign-off is on the agenda.
- [ISO/IEC 42006:2025 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Requirements for bodies providing audit and certification of artificial intelligence management systems](https://www.iso.org/standard/44546.html) — the accreditation requirements for bodies certifying enterprises to ISO/IEC 42001; the standard the analyst reads when distinguishing an *accredited* certification claim from a *self-declared* one on external-facing content.
- [ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 — Artificial intelligence](https://www.iso.org/committee/6794475.html) — the subcommittee page; watch for the ongoing 42001 amendments, 12792 transparency standard, and adjacent 5259 data-quality standards.
- [ISO/IEC 12792 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Transparency taxonomy of AI systems](https://www.iso.org/standard/84111.html) — the transparency-taxonomy standard the external-facing model-card work cross-references. <!-- needs-research: confirm current publication status and any errata against SC 42's live workstream page. -->
- [ISO/IEC 38500:2024 — Information technology — Governance of IT for the organization](https://www.iso.org/standard/81684.html) — the parent IT-governance standard 38507 inherits the *evaluate / direct / monitor* triad from.
- [ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Information security management systems — Requirements](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) — the ISMS standard the third-party AI vendor review standard cross-references for the vendor's baseline-security-posture evidence.

## What is deliberately not here

- Vendor "AI council platform" comparison quadrants — the pre-read shape (chapter 04) and the minutes shape (chapter 05) carry across whichever tool the enterprise runs; the analyst reads whatever GRC platform the enterprise has adopted. A comparison of vendor A against vendor B is marketing-adjacent; the four-artefact-family discipline is what carries across tools.
- Consultancy-branded *AI governance maturity models* that score the enterprise on a five-level scale — a scored maturity narrative is not the analyst's artefact. Maturity assessments belong to `senior-ai-governance-architect` or `head-of-ai-governance` if they belong anywhere.
- Template libraries for AUPs, model-approval standards, or vendor review standards that pre-fill enterprise-specific language — chapter 06 was explicit that policy is enterprise-specific; a template that reads like it landed from a consultancy and is adopted verbatim does not survive first council pass. The universal-shape scaffold (purpose, scope, definitions, requirements, roles, exceptions, enforcement, related, revision-history) is the reusable part; the substantive requirements are the enterprise's to author.
- Snapshots of the AIGP body of knowledge, the CIPP exam blueprints, or the ISO/IEC 42001 lead-implementer training syllabus taken more than one release cycle ago — all three change; the analyst reads the live URL at registration time and marks `<!-- needs-research: -->` where the current state is not verified.
- Vendor claims of *"AI-Act-compliant"* or *"ISO/IEC 42001-certified"* on marketing pages without a linked accreditation-body reference (ANAB, UKAS, or IAF-signatory equivalent) — chapter 07 was explicit that *certified* means a certificate exists from an accredited certification body. Vendor readiness claims without an accreditation trail are marketing, not evidence.
- Practitioner blog posts that use *policy*, *standard*, *guideline*, *guidance*, *rule*, and *procedure* interchangeably — chapter 06 was explicit that the four levels have different scope, length, ratifier, and audience. A blog that flattens them is not the analyst's reference for a first-cut draft's level assignment.
