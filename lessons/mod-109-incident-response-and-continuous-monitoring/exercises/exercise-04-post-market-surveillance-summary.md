# Exercise 04 — Post-Market Surveillance Summary

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Grounding chapters:** [07 — EU AI Act Post-Market Surveillance and Serious-Incident Reporting](../07-eu-ai-act-post-market-surveillance.md), [03 — Logging the Incident Against the Risk Register](../03-incident-record-and-register-linkage.md), [05 — Reading Continuous-Monitoring Dashboards for Governance Sign-off](../05-monitoring-dashboards-for-governance-signoff.md), [09 — Delegation Boundary and Escalation Patterns](../09-delegation-boundary-and-escalation-patterns.md).

## Objective

Compose one worked quarterly post-market surveillance (PMS) summary end-to-end for a high-risk AI system under EU AI Act Article 72, walking the seven-section shape chapter 07 fixes, and — for the same system — draft the standing Article 73 serious-incident notification template with the clock-discipline chapter 07 walks. The drill's point is to *operate the Article-72-and-73 shape* against a plausible high-risk-system scenario with the analyst-tier reading depth chapter 07 fixes: the analyst drafts, the accountable executive signs and submits; the analyst reads the Article 3(49) criteria at intake and triage but does not adjudicate; the deadline clock is calendar days from awareness, not working days from record-open.

After this exercise you should be able to sit down at the start of any quarterly PMS cycle for a high-risk system and, within a working day, produce the PMS summary skeleton populated from the incident records, monitoring-reading notes, escalation register, and change-management records the period surfaced — and, on the day the Article 73 threshold plausibly attaches to a specific incident, produce a notification draft the accountable executive can review inside a same-day window.

## Prerequisites

- Chapter 07 read; the Article 72 obligations (post-market monitoring plan and system; systematic data collection; continued-compliance evaluation; Annex-IV technical documentation reference), the Article 73 obligations (serious-incident reporting; 15-day general / 10-day death / 2-day critical-infrastructure or widespread-infringement calendar-day deadlines), the Article 3(49) definition of *serious incident* across the four outcome classes, the seven-section PMS summary shape (data-collection completeness; performance findings; incidents in the period; escalations in the period; material changes; assessment of continued conformity; actions and follow-through), the Article 73 notification draft shape (header; substantive content; deadline discipline), the provider-vs-deployer role split, and the interaction with GDPR / MDR / IVDR / product-safety parallel reporting fresh in memory.
- Chapter 03 read; the `INC-<year>-<sequence>` row shape (the incidents that populate PMS-summary §3).
- Chapter 05 read; the four-family reading and the monitoring-reading note (the performance data that populates PMS-summary §2).
- Chapter 09 read; the four escalation classes (the escalations that populate PMS-summary §4).
- `mod-102` inventory row for the system you are summarising (see below), `mod-105` FRIA (the *measures* the summary reports the operating evidence for), `mod-106` regulator-tracker row for the EU AI Act obligations (per exercise scope), and `mod-108` control-status report for the period (the controls whose test-rows the summary cross-references).
- The [EU AI Act consolidated text on EUR-Lex (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) open at Articles 3(49), 6, 26, 27, 72, and 73 for the drafting of substantive content and the Article 3(49) walk. Chapter 07 was explicit that the analyst reads the primary text.
- The [European Commission AI Office page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office) open for the current status of Commission implementing acts on the Article 72 plan template and the Article 73 notification format.
- The [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) and the [OECD AI Incidents Monitor](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents) open for the external-corpus cross-references PMS-summary §3 rows may carry.

## Problem statement

The deliverable is a **single Markdown file, `mod-109-ex-04-pms-summary.md`**, containing the worked PMS summary for the system-and-period scenario below plus the Article 73 notification-draft template opened against one incident from the period.

### The scenario (self-contained)

The system under summary is a **CV-screening AI assistant** — a foundation-model-backed classifier that ranks candidate CVs against a job-requisition template, deployed as a hosted service to enterprise-recruiting customers across the EU. Inventory row: `INV-2026-0512` (use your equivalent from `mod-102` if you have carried a hiring-adjacent system forward; otherwise use this placeholder verbatim).

- **EU AI Act classification.** High-risk under Annex III item 4(a) *AI systems intended to be used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons*. The enterprise's role for this system is **provider** (the enterprise placed the system on the Union market under its own name). Certain customer deployments make the enterprise's customers *deployers* subject to Article 26 and — for their public-sector deployments — Article 27 FRIA obligations; the enterprise's PMS plan requires deployer feedback per Article 72.
- **Reporting period.** Q3 2026 (**2026-07-01 through 2026-09-30**).
- **Post-market monitoring plan reference.** `PMS-plan-INV-2026-0512-v2`, owned by the enterprise's EU AI Act compliance function (a joint function of `head-of-ai-governance`, `legal`, and `senior-ai-governance-architect`). The plan requires: quarterly PMS summaries; monthly monitoring-reading notes per chapter 05; incident records per chapter 03; deployer-feedback intake through a portal with a 10-working-day disposition target; material-change records per the enterprise's change-management standard; continued-compliance evaluation against the specific claims in the Annex IV technical documentation.
- **Accountable executive.** `head-of-ai-governance` on the governance side; the enterprise's Chief Product Officer as the signatory on Article 73 notifications per the enterprise's EU AI Act compliance function's designation.
- **Q3-2026 incidents on `INV-2026-0512`.** Three records:
    - `INC-2026-0177` — a class-4 (biased-outcome escalation) incident opened 2026-07-14 after a Dutch customer's HR team surfaced a pattern of the system down-ranking CVs from a specific applicant cohort (older-worker segment); disaggregated re-eval on 2026-08-04 confirmed a demographic-parity ratio outside the FRIA-cited acceptable band on the *age-40-plus* segment; FRIA `FRIA-2026-0512-v3` re-open completed 2026-09-02 with new *measures* on segment-monitoring cadence and *human-review-of-flagged-cohort* for that segment; Article 73 walk: *evaluated — not a serious incident under Article 3(49) at this scope; risk-owner re-accepted residual with the new measures; deployer notification programme launched 2026-09-05*.

- **Q3-2026 escalations on `INV-2026-0512`.** Two records from chapter 06's escalation register:
    - `ESC-2026-0114` — Class 1 model-behaviour escalation to `ai-risk-engineer` on 2026-07-16 arising from `INC-2026-0177`; requested a fresh disaggregated eval on the *age-40-plus* segment; resolved 2026-08-04 with the eval landed and the FRIA re-open triggered.
    - `ESC-2026-0129` — Class 1 model-behaviour escalation to `ai-risk-engineer` on 2026-08-22 arising from `INC-2026-0203`; requested a fresh vendor-relationship review with the foundation-model provider on their silent-update practice; resolved 2026-09-15 with the provider committing to a 30-day advance notice on future model-version updates and the enterprise's contract amended.
- **Q3-2026 monitoring-reading notes on `INV-2026-0512`.** Three notes (one per month) per chapter 05's cadence; the September note carries a *warning* interpretation because of the injection-classifier guardrail deployment and its operating-evidence baseline gap.
- **Q3-2026 material changes on `INV-2026-0512`.** Four records:
    - 2026-07-22 — retrieval-index refresh (routine).
    - 2026-08-22 — model-version pin (in response to `INC-2026-0203`).
    - 2026-09-02 — FRIA update (in response to `INC-2026-0177`).
    - 2026-09-24 — guardrail deployment: system-prompt-injection classifier (in response to `INC-2026-0231`).

    - 2026-08-30 — Italian customer requested the FRIA extract for their own Article 27 obligation; provided within the 10-working-day disposition target.

    - *Human oversight claim.* Every hiring-decision-relevant output must be delivered with the *human-review-recommended* flag and a *cohort-explanation* attachment.
    - *Transparency claim.* Every deployer receives the FRIA extract and the model card at onboarding and on any material change.

### Step 2 — Compose §2 through §7 of the PMS summary (§2–§7)

Author each of the seven sections per chapter 07's shape:

- **§2 Data collection completeness.** One paragraph per data source: continuous monitoring (chapter 05); deployer feedback (Article 72); incident records (chapter 03); escalations (chapter 06); material changes; third-party findings. Gaps are named explicitly and cross-referenced to any `mod-108` control-testing finding that captures the gap. The Q3-2026 scenario has one gap-of-note candidate — the human-review-overturn-rate metric from exercise 03 was without a baseline in July and August; if it is still without a baseline in September, the finding lives here.
- **§3 Performance findings.** One paragraph per continued-compliance criterion. Cross-reference the underlying data to the monitoring-reading notes (`MON-2026-*`). The scenario provides five continued-compliance criteria (accuracy, fairness, safety, human oversight, transparency); every one gets a paragraph with the specific reading against the specific claim.
- **§4 Incidents in the period.** Populate the shape for each of the three incidents (`INC-2026-0177`, `INC-2026-0203`, `INC-2026-0231`) — incident ID and class, Article 3(49) evaluation with rationale, Article 73 notification status (with the identifier `A73-*` where the draft opened, even if not submitted), control-status changes triggered (from `mod-108`), IA / FRIA / DPIA re-open status.
- **§5 Escalations in the period.** Populate the shape for each of the two escalations (`ESC-2026-0114`, `ESC-2026-0129`) — class, owner, current status, resolution artefact identifier.
- **§6 Material changes in the period.** Populate the shape for each of the four material changes — date, actor, cross-reference to the change-management record, and the effect on continued-compliance claims (the guardrail deployment affects the safety claim; the model-version pin affects the accuracy claim; the FRIA update affects the fairness and human-oversight claims; the retrieval-index refresh has no direct claim effect).
- **§7 Assessment of continued conformity.** The accountable executive's determination — for signature — per chapter 07's five options (*conformity maintained* / *conformity maintained with named observations* / *conformity in question — corrective action open* / *non-conformity identified — corrective action complete before period close* / *non-conformity identified — corrective action in flight past period close*). The analyst drafts; the executive signs. The scenario's Q3 pattern (one biased-outcome incident with resolved FRIA re-open; one model regression with resolved rollback and vendor commitment; one injection-exploit with resolved guardrail deployment and *not-submitted* Article 73 draft) fits the *conformity maintained with named observations* posture; state the observations explicitly with the follow-through owner.

Add the **§8 Actions and follow-through** section per chapter 07 — corrective and preventive actions from §3, §4, §5, and §7 with owners and target dates; hand-off to the next-period summary for any action carrying over (the Bundesnetzagentur questionnaire response, the injection-classifier guardrail's second-quarter operating-evidence baseline, the vendor-commitment 30-day-notice compliance monitoring).

Add the **Attachments** block per chapter 07 — the monitoring-reading notes for the period, the incident records, the escalation records, the change-management records, and any deployer-supplied feedback records.

## Requirements

- **Every field of chapter 07's PMS summary shape is populated** — the header table, §2 through §7 (plus §8 actions block), the attachments block. Blank sections are not permitted; where the period has no items in a section (e.g., zero third-party findings other than the Bundesnetzagentur questionnaire), name the state explicitly (*"no other third-party findings in the period"*).
- **The Article 3(49) walk is complete for every incident in §4.** All four outcome classes (death or serious harm to health; critical-infrastructure disruption; fundamental-rights infringement; serious harm to property or environment) are named per incident with an *implicated* / *not-implicated — <rationale>* determination. Chapter 07 was explicit: silent *not-triggered* is a fail; the walk records the rationale per class.
- **The Article 73 notification status is set per incident in §4** — *not-applicable* / *drafted* / *submitted*. `INC-2026-0231` is *drafted-not-submitted* with the notification identifier `A73-2026-INV-2026-0512-0001`; `INC-2026-0177` and `INC-2026-0203` are *not-applicable* with the Article 3(49) walk rationale.
- **§7 continued-conformity assessment cites the specific claims from the Annex IV technical documentation.** *Accuracy*, *fairness*, *safety*, *human oversight*, *transparency* per the scenario. Each claim gets a determination *maintained* / *maintained-with-observation* / *in-question* / *non-conformity*.
- **§7 lands the accountable executive's determination as *conformity maintained with named observations* with the three observations named.** The scenario is engineered for this determination; a *conformity maintained* without observations reads the scenario as tighter than it is; a *conformity in question* over-reads the incident set given the resolved status of each.
- **The Article 73 notification draft in §9 is complete in substance.** Header block populated (with placeholders where the scenario does not supply); substantive content covers the six chapter-07-named topics (serious incident, Article 3(49) walk, causal-link status, scope of impact, AI system, containment, corrective actions, coordination with other authorities, attachments); deadline discipline named against the 2026-09-18 awareness date.
- **The Article 73 deadline is measured in calendar days, not working days.** Chapter 07 was explicit: the deadlines in Article 73 are calendar days. Any calculation in §9 that says *the 15-day path expires on 2026-10-05* (working-days calculation) is off-side; *the 15-day path expires on 2026-10-03* (calendar-days from 2026-09-18) is correct.
- **The provider-vs-deployer role split is stated explicitly on §1 and §9.** The enterprise is the *provider* on this system; the enterprise's customers are the *deployers* subject to Article 26 (and Article 27 for public-sector deployments). The obligations differ; the drafting reflects the difference.
- **The GDPR coordination is stated on §9 for `INC-2026-0231`.** The CV data is personal data; the injection-exploit did not exfiltrate or expose the personal data in the enterprise's technical infrastructure; the coordination with the DPO is recorded even though no GDPR Article 33 notification attaches. Chapter 07 was explicit: parallel reporting is cross-referenced, not run.
- **The sign-off cover in §10 requests specific actions.** The executive's signature on §7 and the counter-sign on the `INC-2026-0231` no-submission determination. A cover that requests *the executive review the summary* without naming specific sign-off actions is off-side.
- **No fabricated regulator names or authority identifiers.** Use the placeholder pattern for authority names where the scenario does not provide one (*the designated market surveillance authority for [Member State]*, resolved via the enterprise's compliance-function tracker per `mod-106`).
- **No fabricated deployer names.** The scenario names *Dutch customer*, *German customer*, *Italian customer*, *French customer* without specific enterprise names; the draft preserves the same anonymity.

If the draft cites either, mark the citation with `<!-- needs-research: verify current -->` per the module's discipline.

## Starter guidance

- Do §1 through §7 in order. Chapter 07's shape is not chronological accident — the completeness of §2 (data collection) is what §3 (performance findings) reads against; the incidents in §4 are what §7 (continued-conformity assessment) weighs. Compose top-to-bottom; do not skip to §7 and back-fill.
- The Article 3(49) walk in §4 is the load-bearing analyst action on this drill. Chapter 07 was explicit: *silent Article 3(49) evaluation is a common failure mode*. For each incident, walk all four outcome classes with a rationale for *not-implicated* even when the class is obviously not implicated. The class-1 model regression is the trap — the rationale for *not-implicated* on all four classes needs to state *the enterprise's customers' human-review workflows caught any ranking errors during the affected window*, not just *task-completion drop does not implicate serious incidents*.
- `INC-2026-0231` (the injection-exploit) is the specific incident where the Article 73 notification draft opens. The scenario's engineering matters: 40% reproducibility is high enough that *suspected causal link* is credible; the enterprise cannot demonstrate that no realised harm occurred without deployer support; the 15-day path applies; the executive's *no-submission* determination is defensible only with `legal` counsel review; the notification draft remains in the incident record for audit reference regardless. Chapter 07's *the clock starts before anyone tells the executive* failure mode is what the drill's design tests.
- The continued-conformity assessment in §7 is the executive's signature-line. The analyst drafts the assessment with the specific claim-by-claim reading and names the observations; the executive's signature turns the draft into the assertion. Draft honestly — the September groundedness gap from exercise 03 (if you carry it forward) is a *maintained-with-observation* case, not a *maintained* case. Chapter 07 was explicit that soft-pedalling to *maintained* is a chapter-07 failure mode.
- The sign-off cover in §10 is a small artefact but a load-bearing one. It is the artefact the executive reads first; if it reads well, the executive's PMS-summary review is fast; if it does not, the executive reads through the summary looking for the surprises. Frame it as the two-page memo an executive who reviews thirty of these a quarter can absorb in two minutes.
- The Bundesnetzagentur questionnaire is a *hand-off to the compliance function*, not an action the analyst takes on the PMS summary. Chapter 07 was explicit: the analyst reads the plan; the plan itself is authored by the compliance function; the market-surveillance-authority engagement is a compliance-function action. The PMS summary records the questionnaire, hands it off, and does not attempt to draft the response.
- Where the scenario does not name a specific date (e.g., *composed on*), pick the first working week of October 2026; where the scenario does not name a specific person (e.g., the Chief Product Officer's actual name), use a placeholder (*[CPO-name-per-enterprise-compliance-function]*). Chapter 07's discipline is not to invent people.
